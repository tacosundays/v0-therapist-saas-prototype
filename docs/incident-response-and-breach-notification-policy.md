# Incident Response and Breach Notification Policy

Effective date: October 6, 2026  
Owner: SessionSteps LLC  
Status: Launch draft pending owner approval and qualified legal/security review.

## Report and triage

Report suspected security or privacy incidents immediately to security@sessionsteps.com. Do not include PHI in an initial ordinary-email report. Record the time discovered, reporter, affected systems, suspected data, and current evidence. Classify severity based on confidentiality, integrity, availability, number of people, clinical impact, and ongoing attacker access.

## Containment and preservation

- Revoke exposed sessions, rotate affected credentials, isolate compromised systems, and disable risky integrations without destroying evidence.
- Preserve relevant logs, timestamps, configuration, messages, and deployment records with access restricted to responders.
- Use the production rollback and recovery procedures when they reduce harm.

## Investigation and communication

- Determine what happened, when, affected people/workspaces, data involved, acquisition or viewing, and whether the event is still active.
- Notify affected customer contacts promptly when their information may be involved and cooperate with their investigation.
- Only an authorized owner or advisor communicates externally. Do not speculate or promise that an event is or is not a legal breach before assessment.

## Notification decision

The owner coordinates with qualified counsel to assess obligations under HIPAA, contracts, state breach laws, and any applicable FTC requirements. Document the decision, supporting risk assessment, recipients, content, delivery method, and timing. Contractual deadlines may be shorter than statutory outer limits and must be followed.

## Recovery and lessons learned

Restore safely, validate access and data integrity, increase monitoring, and obtain customer confirmation where appropriate. Within ten business days of containment, document root cause, actions, remaining risks, policy or technical changes, owners, and deadlines. Test this procedure through a tabletop exercise at least annually.
